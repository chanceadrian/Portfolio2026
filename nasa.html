When Earth Is Out of Reach

Crew must think, decide, and act—on their own.

As human spaceflight moves beyond low-Earth orbit, communication delays and blackouts make real-time ground support impossible, requiring crews to manage complex, unanticipated situations independently.

In response, NASA's Earth-Independent Operations effort is developing integrated onboard systems that bring monitoring, reasoning, and action directly to the crew—shifting problem-solving from Earth to the spacecraft. Early progress focuses on proving that these technologies can reduce risk and enable confident, timely decisions when it matters most.

Anomaly Response

Informed and ready to act.

Anomaly response is a sequence of evolving judgments made under uncertainty. The real challenge is understanding what changed, what caused it, and how urgently it matters. As missions move farther from Earth, this process must shift onboard — supported by interfaces that help crews see not just what is happening, but why it matters and what to do next.

  • Overview. Understand the current state of the anomaly, including impacts, system status, and what requires attention now. Designed to help crews establish context quickly.

  • Impact. Understand how long the vehicle can safely operate before conditions worsen or capabilities are affected. Helps crews prioritize response activities and manage urgency.

  • Evidence. Review the observable signals forming the anomaly's signature, including telemetry shifts, advisories, and changing system behavior.

  • Contributing Factors. Identify system conditions, operational context, and historical patterns that may help explain the anomaly or influence its progression.

  • Possible cause. Explore plausible causes derived from available evidence. New information may strengthen, weaken, or rule out explanations over time.

  • Procedures. Find a list of procedures that can help you verify or correct any known cause.
